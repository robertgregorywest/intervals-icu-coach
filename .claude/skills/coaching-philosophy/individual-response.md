# Individual response

Athletes differ in what works. Some arrive with a big natural base and move little with training;
others start lower and absorb load well. Training studies show it plainly: the same programme
produces a wide spread of gains, and about half of that spread runs in families (Bouchard et al.,
1999). A one-size-fits-all plan fits the average athlete, and nobody is the average athlete.

So coaching is applied experimentation: start from the best general evidence, measure how this
athlete responds, and keep what works for them.

## Defaults are priors

Every pillar, rule and bias in this skill is a starting point — the best bet before this athlete's
response is known. The athlete's own history is evidence and, once read carefully, outranks the
population average. A post-hoc read of past seasons is good for _forming_ a hypothesis; it carries
every confounder those seasons had, so a prospective experiment is what _confirms_ it.

A default the athlete's evidence contradicts is changed through `docs/personal/steering.md` as an
override or an experiment, never by quietly drifting from it.

## A change of approach is an experiment

When the question is "does this work for this athlete?", run it as an experiment, not a hunch. It
needs, written down before it starts:

- **Hypothesis** — what changes, and what it should move.
- **Dose** — what must be delivered for the result to count, and how it is tracked.
- **Readouts** — the tests and metrics that judge it, on a fixed protocol.
- **Success threshold** — the size of change that counts, set in advance: bigger than test-to-test
  noise and big enough to matter on the target event.
- **Judging date** — at the end of an adaptation timescale, not when a result first looks good or
  bad.

Live experiments are recorded in `steering.md`; judged ones leave it as a belief or a closed log
line.

## Experimentation runs inside consistency

The risk with an experimental mindset is fiddling: a new idea every three weeks, nothing run long
enough to read, and the athlete's rhythm broken by the coach's curiosity. Consistency is not in
tension with experimentation — it is what makes an experiment readable. These rules keep the two
together:

- **The base pattern is fixed; the experiment is one dial.** The minimum viable week, recovery
  weeks, execution rules and test cadence hold throughout. An experiment changes _what fills_ the
  plan — a dose, a session shape — not whether the plan holds. If the question is about one of
  those rules, it becomes an explicit steering override for the experiment's duration.
- **One training experiment at a time.** Two changes at once confound each other and neither can
  be judged. An observational thread — reading a metric without changing training for it — is not
  an experiment and can run alongside.
- **Run it to the judging date.** Engine changes take weeks to months; a block in which nothing has
  moved yet is not a verdict. Not-yet-working is never a reason to switch. Stop early only for
  harm — deteriorating readiness, illness, injury — and record the stop as such.
- **Adjusting is not experimenting.** Day-to-day auto-regulation (moving a session, trimming a set
  on a poor-readiness day) keeps the experiment on course and does not reset it. Changing the
  hypothesis, the dose or the readouts mid-run does: that is a new experiment with a new start
  date.
- **Experiments shape blocks, not sessions.** A session exists for the block goal; the experiment
  explains why the block is shaped as it is. No session is scheduled only to answer a question.

## Reading the result

- **Not delivered is not refuted.** If the dose did not happen, the experiment did not run — say so
  and do not read the result. Much apparent non-response is under-dosing: "non-responders" to
  endurance training respond when the dose is raised (Montero & Lundby, 2017).
- **A flat result after a delivered dose** points at the stimulus before the athlete. A high-base,
  low-absorption athlete may need a bigger or different stimulus, not more patience.
- **Name the confounders.** Illness, life stress, heat, season, equipment and any other change in
  training are logged as they happen, so the judgement can weigh them rather than discover them.
- **Triangulate.** Several readouts moving the same way outweigh one test. One test is one sample,
  with its day's noise.
- **Every outcome is recorded.** Supported → a steering belief, graduating here once proven
  across seasons. Refuted → closed with what was learned. Not delivered → untested, re-run or
  dropped. Ambiguous → extended or redesigned once, explicitly, with a new judging date.

Measurement is what makes all of this possible: without regular testing on a repeatable protocol
the coach cannot tell what a block did. → [testing.md](testing.md)

Sources: Bouchard, C. et al. (1999). Familial aggregation of VO2max response to exercise training:
results from the HERITAGE Family Study. _Journal of Applied Physiology_, 87(3). Montero, D. &
Lundby, C. (2017). Refuting the myth of non-response to exercise training: 'non-responders' do
respond to higher dose of training. _Journal of Physiology_, 595(11).
