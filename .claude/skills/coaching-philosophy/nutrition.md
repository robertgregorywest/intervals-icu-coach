# Nutrition

Fuel the work required. Carbs periodised with session demand — high on long/hard days, scaled back
on easy days. Hydration with electrolytes; train the gut alongside the legs.

Under-fuelling blunts adaptation more than it produces leanness — and it costs the rest of the day
too: mood, patience and the next session.

## Protein — the masters requirement

- **1.6–2.2 g/kg/day**, the top end in hard blocks or when holding muscle in a deficit. The 0.8 g/kg
  adult baseline is too low for a masters athlete (Stern, 2026a). Duchy put the masters baseline at
  ~1.8 g/kg/day, rising to ≥2.0 in high-volume or high-intensity blocks (Moore, 2021; Doering, 2017).
- **Spread evenly across 4–5 feeds**, not loaded at dinner (Stern, 2026a; Moore, 2021). Ageing muscle
  responds less to a given dose (anabolic resistance; Burd, 2013), and the response comes back only
  above a higher per-dose threshold (Churchward-Venne, 2016). So it is the size of each feed that
  matters, not one big shake.
- **30–40 g within two hours of a hard session** (Stern, 2026a), and protein after the gym as well
  as after the ride on stacked days.

## Carbohydrate around rides

- **Before a long or hard ride:** 150–200 g carbohydrate 2–3 h before the start (Stern, 2026b). Before
  an early session, when a meal isn't possible, have something rather than nothing: a smoothie made
  the night before, or juice. Don't ride fasted into a working day. It raises the stress cost of
  both (Duchy, no source given).
- **During rides ≥ 2 h:** **80–90 g/hr** from a glucose–fructose mix; **60 g/hr is the floor, not
  the target**, for sustained tempo work of around three hours (Stern, 2026b). Higher intakes protect
  late-ride power: in a 2026 _Scand J Med Sci Sports_ study of trained amateurs, 120 g/hr roughly
  halved the threshold-power fade after 3 h compared with 60 g/hr (11 W against 20 W; cited by
  Duchy, not yet read in full). Build towards higher rates over weeks; never jump straight to them.
- **Cold rides cost more**: thermoregulation raises energy cost and adds to total load (Blondin,
  2015; Castellani & Tipton, 2016; via Duchy). Fuel above the usual rate in winter.
- **After:** 30 g protein + 30 g carbs straight away, ≥50 g carbs within 30 min, a full meal within
  2 h (Stern, 2026b). Eating 20–30 g carbs + 15–25 g protein in the last 30 min of a long ride
  heads off the post-ride binge (Duchy, no source given).
- **Fuel the whole day, not just the ride.** A morning ride followed by work or family needs both
  covered. Going to bed hungry disrupts sleep, and short sleep shifts the appetite hormones the
  next day (Spiegel, 2004; via Duchy).

## Supplements

- **Creatine monohydrate 3–5 g/day** is a reasonable masters option, no loading phase needed
  (Stern, 2026a). It is not a substitute for the protein target.
