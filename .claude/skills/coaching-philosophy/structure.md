# Structure

Sessions are part of a bigger picture, not isolated rides. Default to a **pyramidal mix** — bulk of
time at endurance (Z2), regular MIET/sweetspot to build FTP and muscular endurance, a smaller but
critical dose of high-intensity (VO₂max, anaerobic).

**The middle of the pyramid is load-bearing, and it has a floor.** The middle is the **76–106% FTP**
band — tempo, sweetspot and threshold together, not just sweetspot-and-above. In build blocks, two
tiers, both protected before anything else when time is short:

- **3–4 sessions per week containing a work block of ≥5 min** in that band. This is the bulk of the
  stimulus and the tier that degrades first.
- **of which 1–1.5 contain a ≥20 min continuous block.** Fewer than the shorter sessions by design —
  a target of 2–3 per week is more sustained continuous work than a well-trained masters athlete
  reliably delivers, and setting it there guarantees a missed target every week.

"Regular MIET/sweetspot" left unquantified is how a pyramidal plan silently degrades into
Z2-plus-a-little-HIT while volume and CTL look healthy — the 20–60 min engine decays on a dashboard
that says fitness is up. The specific failure mode to watch for is **polarisation by subtraction**:
the middle band is evacuated, the hours reappear as endurance, the HIT dose never changes, and total
volume and CTL both _rise_. Nothing in a load-based dashboard flags it.

**Build the middle from below.** Most in-band time sits at tempo (76–88% FTP), below sweetspot — in
the athlete's strongest years, about two-thirds of it. Heavy-domain work buys the most accumulable
dose per unit of recovery for a masters athlete, and pursuit/MAP work keeps the ceiling trained, so
fractional utilisation is what the middle builds. Sustained _supra_threshold work (over-unders,
100–110% FTP) is a specific-prep and pre-TT delivery tool, not a base ingredient.

**When time is short, cut hours and keep the dose.** A week that holds its in-band time on less
riding keeps most of the engine — an efficient fallback, not a free lunch, since base hours on top
add something of their own. The reverse trade, hours kept and in-band time cut, is polarisation by
subtraction: power falls at every duration from 20 min up while CTL rises.

**The ceiling is never left untouched.** Base blocks carry no structured VO₂ block, but a small
weekly touch stays: hill efforts at the bottom of L6 inside a hard-day ride early on, then
short-rep, long-recovery work before the main VO₂ block (Stern's over-40s sequence). When training
is reduced, intensity is what holds VO₂max (Hickson et al., 1985, _J Appl Physiol_ 58:492), and
masters decline tracks the training kept up (Burtscher et al., 2022, _IJERPH_ 19:11050).

In build blocks, HIT/track work is a _maintenance_ dose and the hard-day budget goes to the sustained
middle; specific-prep and sharpening blocks deliberately flip that emphasis, and the last four weeks before
an A race invert the build rules outright ([peaking.md](peaking.md)).

**Count the band, not just the session.** A session count can be met with scattered surges that
never accumulate. Track in-band time per week as the primary number and the session counts as the
shape — and note that a threshold-band target only means what its **block-length floor** says it
means: the same week can read two- to three-fold differently at a 2-min floor versus a 5-min floor.
State the floor whenever the target is stated.

Anchor hard days when fresh; place key work before long. Progress **one variable at a time**
(duration _or_ intensity, not both).

## Vary the format, not the dose

The same session at the same workload, repeated indefinitely, plateaus (Stern, 2026). It is a
bigger risk for a masters athlete, whose response to an unchanging stimulus fades sooner (Duchy
Coaching's argument from anabolic resistance; plausible, but not settled). The answer is to change
the _shape_ of the work while holding its _dose_:

- **Rotate formats inside a target band every block (or 2–3 weeks)**, and give the two weekly
  sessions in that band different formats. Middle-band examples: continuous tempo, tempo with
  short surges, low-cadence tempo, tempo with alternating cadence blocks, sweetspot inside a Z2
  ride, a long tempo block late in a long ride. The band time and its block-length floor stay the
  metric. A format change is not a progression, so it does not count as this week's one variable.
- **Change with a reason.** The levers are intensity, volume, recovery and session structure.
  Making every session harder because another week has passed is not progression (Stern, 2026).
  Variety never overrides what a session is for: a key session keeps its target, and an experiment
  keeps its dose.
- **Vary the low-cost work too**: sprint type (seated, standing, rolling), cadence, terrain and
  route.
